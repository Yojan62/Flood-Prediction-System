The Evolution of a Flood Prediction Model

A guide to the data science behind the Bangladesh FloodWarning App

The Goal: Predict the Unpredictable

The objective was to build a Machine Learning model that could accurately predict river discharge (the volume of water) 24 hours in the future. The challenge isn't just predicting the *normal* flow, but accurately predicting the **dangerous, sudden flood peaks** that cause the most damage.

Attempt 1: The "Generalist" Model (v10)

Our first model (v10) was a stacked ensemble of powerful tree-based models (RandomForest, GradientBoosting). It was trained on 11 features, including rain, temperature, and soil moisture.

Simple Analogy

Think of the v10 model as a "B Student." It's very smart and gets a 95% on the test by acing all the easy, day-to-day questions. But when it gets to the 5% of "extra-hard" questions (the sudden flood peaks), it guesses and gets them all wrong. It's reliable, but not for the events that matter most.

Technical Detail

The v10 model used a standard `log1p` transform on the target (`y`) and was trained with `sample_weight` to focus on peaks. However, it still suffered from a common problem: the model's loss function optimized for the *overall* error. Because 90% of the data is "normal" flow, the model learned to be extremely good at predicting "normal" and simply "smoothed over" the rare, chaotic flood spikes. This resulted in a high overall R2 score but a **negative R2_Peak score**.

Result: The v10 model (red) was excellent at predicting the general flow but consistently **under-predicted** the flood peaks (blue).

Attempt 2: The "Two-Stage" Hybrid (v15)

Since one model couldn't do both jobs, we built a hybrid system with two models working together: a "Baseflow" model and a "Residual" (Spike-Predictor) model.

Simple Analogy

We hired a **team of two specialists**.

  1. **The "Baseflow" Expert (LightGBM):** This model's *only* job is to predict the slow, easy, day-to-day river level.
  2. **The "Spike" Expert (LSTM):** This neural network's *only* job is to study the "error" from the first model. It learns to predict the fast, chaotic spikes that the first model misses.

The final prediction is **(Expert 1's Prediction) + (Expert 2's Prediction)**.

Technical Detail

This is a **hybrid residual model**.

  1. A `LightGBM` model is trained on the full tabular feature set to predict the `log1p(target)`. This creates a stable but "smoothed" baseflow prediction.
  2. We calculate the error of this model (the "residuals"): `observed_flow - baseflow_prediction`.
  3. A **Bidirectional LSTM** (a neural network) is then trained *only* on the sequence features (lags, rain) to predict these residuals. This allows the LSTM to focus exclusively on the non-linear, temporal "spike" dynamics that the tree-based model failed to capture.

The final prediction is `np.expm1(lgbm_pred) + lstm_residual_pred`.

The Result: v15 Predicts the Peaks

This chart compares the performance of both models against the real-world data from the 2024-2025 test period. The v15 hybrid model's ability to capture the flood spikes is a dramatic improvement.

The Final Scoreboard: Why v15 Wins

The metrics clearly show the story. While v10 was "good on average," it failed at its most important job. v15 succeeds at both.

Simple Analogy

  • R2 Score (Overall Grade): How well the model did on the *entire* test. v10 got a 95%, but v15 got a 96% by also acing the hard questions.
  • R2 Peak Score (The "Flood" Grade): How well the model did on *only* the hard flood questions. v10 got an "F" (a negative score), while v15 got a "C+" (a positive score). A passing grade on the hard questions is a massive victory.

Technical Detail

  • R2 Score (Coefficient of Determination): The proportion of variance explained by the model. A score of 1.0 is perfect. v15's 0.963 is a world-class result for general flow.
  • R2 Peak Score:** A custom metric. We calculate R2 *only* on data points above the 90th percentile (the flood level). v10's negative score (`-0.28`) means it was worse than useless. v15's positive score (`+0.35`) proves it has real, predictive skill during the most critical events.
